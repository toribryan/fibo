# Pixel snail

A one-color pixel-art snail that crawls on a loop while something loads. As
a sprite inside your own SVG, he can also build himself up, dance and turn
to watch the pointer.

<SnailCover />

## Features

- A four-frame crawl with head, shell and tail on different beats, so his
  foot stretches and gathers instead of sliding.
- Three sprite modes, `crawl`, `rest` and `dance`, each a hand-timed loop.
- A build-up from coarse blocks to fine pixels, with a callback per step
  for syncing sound.
- `look` stops him and turns his eyes, head and neck toward a point, and
  his whole body when it's behind him.

## Usage

```tsx
import { PixelSnail } from "@/components/ui/pixel-snail"

export function ProjectsLoading() {
  return <PixelSnail label="Loading your projects" />
}
```

`PixelSnail` is the loading indicator. `PixelSnailSprite` is a bare `<g>` for
your own SVG. Its origin is under the middle of his foot, so a `transform` to
a point stands him on it.

```tsx
import { PixelSnailSprite } from "@/components/ui/pixel-snail"

export function SnailOnALine() {
  return (
    <svg viewBox="0 0 200 40" className="text-foreground">
      <line x1={0} y1={30} x2={200} y2={30} stroke="currentColor" />
      <PixelSnailSprite
        transform="translate(100 30)"
        pixel={1.5}
        mode="dance"
      />
    </svg>
  )
}
```

## The loading snail

fibo's former mascot, now its loader. The mascot is the pixel rabbit on the <DocLink to="welcome--docs" className="text-foreground underline underline-offset-4">Welcome page</DocLink>,
since the Fibonacci sequence started as a puzzle about rabbits. The snail has
one color and a googly eye on each stalk; his character is in how he moves
and responds.

## How he moves

1. **Grid.** Drawn in whole art pixels with
   `shape-rendering: crispEdges`, and moves only by whole art pixels, even
   when traveling, so he never blurs between two.
2. **Hand timing.** Each loop is a script of frames and holds, not an easing
   curve. The holds are uneven, because an even beat reads as a machine.
3. **One color.** He draws in `currentColor`, so a text class recolors him.
   His eye whites take the page background, keeping his pupils readable in
   either theme.

### The crawl

Four frames. The head reaches forward while the eye stalks tip ahead. Then
the shell slides up while the tail holds back, and the head settles as the
stalks straighten. A cycle moves him forward two art pixels.

In place, a dotted ground slides under him so he still reads as moving. With
`travel` he crosses the container, entering past the left edge and leaving
past the right.

`pace` sets how long each frame holds: 110 ms fast, 180 ms by default and
260 ms slow.

<Canvas of={Stories.Travel} />

### Resting and dancing

Two more sprite loops, each a script of poses and holds.

- **Rest** runs about eleven seconds, with holds from 140 ms to 1.8 seconds.
  He looks about, reaches, blinks and shuffles a pixel forward and back, so
  he never ends a gesture where it began.
- **Dance** is a countable beat. His stalks sway every 280 ms while his shell
  hops a row, three times. Then he shuffles a step aside and back and holds
  for 1.6 seconds, so it never turns frantic.

<Canvas of={Stories.Modes} />

### The build-up

With `assembleDelay`, he loads in like an image over a slow connection
instead of fading in:

- **0 to 400 ms:** 4-pixel blocks arrive in a scattered but fixed order. A
  block is filled when enough of the pixels it covers are.
- **400 to 520 ms:** the full coarse silhouette holds.
- **520 to 680 ms:** the blocks halve to 2 pixels.
- **680 ms:** the real art appears, eyes and all.

The stage comes from elapsed time since the delay, not timer ticks, so a
throttled background tab skips ahead instead of dragging. `onAssemble`
reports each step once: the block size and the share shown, in tenths, then
`"whole"`. Use it to sync sound, such as a blip that climbs in pitch as he
fills in, a brighter one as the blocks split, and a chord at the end.

## How he responds

### Looking at things

`look` stops the current loop and poses him toward a point. Each axis takes
`-1`, `0` or `1`, because pixel art can't show a finer angle.

- **`x`**: behind, level or ahead. Pupils move to that side, stalks lean,
  and the head reaches forward or pulls back a pixel.
- **`y`**: up, level or down. Eyes rise on longer stalks and the neck
  stretches a row, or eyes sink and the head ducks.
- **`facing`**: at `-1` the sprite mirrors to face left.

`null` returns him to his mode.

The sprite doesn't read the pointer, so it also works for following a caret
or watching a drop target. To follow the pointer, map it into art pixels
with some slack:

- **Dead zone around the eyes.** He glances only once the pointer is more
  than 1.5 art pixels off his eye line, so his pupils don't flicker as it
  crosses the middle.
- **Wider gap before turning.** He turns only when the pointer is more than
  4 art pixels past his middle, and keeps his facing inside that gap, so he
  doesn't flip back and forth on hover.

<Canvas of={Stories.FollowsThePointer} />

```tsx
const EYES = { x: 6, y: -11 } // his eyes, from the sprite's origin
const GLANCE = 1.5
const TURN = 4

const glance = (offset: number) =>
  offset < -GLANCE ? -1 : offset > GLANCE ? 1 : 0

// dx and dy: the pointer in art pixels from the sprite's origin
const facing = dx < -TURN ? -1 : dx > TURN ? 1 : previous.facing
const look = {
  facing,
  x: glance((dx - facing * EYES.x) * facing),
  y: glance(dy - EYES.y),
}
```
