# Chapter scrubber

A rail of marks, one per chapter, that swell under the pointer like the macOS
Dock. The tallest mark, the crest, previews its chapter, so people can jump
around a long piece without a table of contents.

## Usage

<UsageGuidelines
  guidelines={[
    "Use it for content with a real sequence: a talk, a long read, a case study, a recording.",
    "Keep between six and thirty chapters. Fewer reads as a list; more stops the wave from reaching the neighbours.",
    <>
      Pick <code>card</code> when chapters have descriptions, <code>label</code>{" "}
      when the title says enough, and <code>none</code> when the page already
      names the section.
    </>,
    <>
      Leave <code>side</code> unset in most layouts. It prefers right or top and
      flips on its own near the viewport edge.
    </>,
    <>
      Set <code>currentIndex</code> from your scroll or playback position so the
      marker keeps up with the reader between clicks.
    </>,
  ]}

<Tip>
  The rail is only as wide as the crest, so it fits in a gutter. Put it in a{" "}
  <code>sticky</code> column beside the content, as in the article example
  below.
</Tip>

## Variants

### Horizontal

A rail along the top of a player or a slide deck. Ticks stand up from the
baseline and the preview opens above.

### Dots

Dots instead of ticks, for a softer rail that sits well beside imagery.

## Do's and don'ts

<ComponentRules
  rules={[
    {
      positive: {
        component: (
          <ChapterScrubber
            preview="none"

      negative: {

        description:
          "Don't use it for three items. Plain links say more in less space.",

## Props

Every visual choice is a prop: orientation, mark style, density, which side
the preview opens toward, and what the preview shows. The numeric props
override the `size` preset when you need an exact rhythm.
