## When to use it

Use Pixel snail for noticeable waits where character helps, such as an empty
panel filling or a first load. `PixelSnail` crawls in place over a dotted
ground as a status region announcing its `label`. `PixelSnailSprite` is a
bare `<g>` for your own SVG, where he can also build himself up, dance and
turn to watch the pointer. No animation library: every frame is state and a
timer, drawn as SVG rectangles.

## Sprite modes

`PixelSnailSprite` has three hand-timed loops: `crawl`, `rest` and `dance`.
Its origin is under the middle of his foot, so a `transform` to a point
stands him on it.

  <PixelSnailModesDemo />
</Preview>

## Following the pointer

`look` stops the current loop and poses him toward a point. Each axis takes
`-1`, `0` or `1`, and `facing` at `-1` mirrors him to face left; `null`
returns him to his mode. The sprite doesn't read the pointer itself, so this
example maps it into art pixels with a dead zone around the eyes and a wider
gap before he turns, so he doesn't flicker or flip on hover.

## Accessibility

- `PixelSnail` is a `status` region holding `label` as visually hidden text,
  since screen readers read a status region's content, not its name. The
  drawing is hidden from assistive technology.
- `PixelSnailSprite` is decoration. Hide the SVG it sits in with
  `aria-hidden`, and put anything he says or means somewhere a screen reader
  can reach it.
- Under prefers-reduced-motion he holds his resting pose: no crawl, travel,
  rest or dance loop, and no build-up. He still turns to look, since that
  only responds to the visitor.
- He isn't focusable.
