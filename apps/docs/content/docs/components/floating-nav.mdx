## When to use it

Use Floating nav for the three to five most-visited destinations on a phone.
An indicator slides to the current item, which expands to show its label;
the others stay as icons. It floats over the page clear of the home
indicator, or sits in the flow with `position="static"`, and with
`hideOnScroll` it hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up or focus.
Items are links or buttons, controlled or uncontrolled, so it works with any
router.

It installs `motion` with it, for the indicator and slide.

## Liquid glass

`variant="glass"` renders a clear bar that bends the page behind its edges,
with a glass lens on the current item. In Chromium the page behind the rim
bends through an SVG displacement filter; Safari and Firefox keep the blur
and rim. A press lifts the lens, which travels to the new item and settles.
Under reduced motion it stays put. The glass bar doesn't clip, so it can't
scroll sideways when items overflow: keep it to items that fit, or use
`size="sm"` on narrow screens.

## Labels always shown

`labels="always"` stacks every label under its icon, like a tab bar. For
icons that are ambiguous alone.

## Controlled links

Items with an `href` render as links. `onValueChange` receives the click
event, so the app can call `event.preventDefault()` and route it.

## Accessibility

- The root is a `nav` landmark named Main, or by `aria-label`. Name it when a
  page has more than one.
- The current item carries `aria-current="page"` when it is a link, or
  `aria-current="true"` when it is a button. Hidden labels stay in the DOM, so
  every item keeps its name.
- Items are plain links and buttons in the tab order. Tabbing into a hidden
  bar brings it back.
- Glass label contrast depends on what scrolls beneath. Use glass over images
  and media, the default bar over dense text.
- Items are at least 44px square, or 36px with `size="sm"`, above the 24px
  minimum target. Under reduced motion the indicator and bar move without
  animating.
