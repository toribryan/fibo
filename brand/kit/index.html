A library of parts for experimental projects and special components that anyone can use. This page is the short version of how fibo looks and talks, plus the first ten posts, ready to go.

01

What fibo is

An achromatic design system on shadcn/ui and Base UI. Every part is documented in Storybook and installs from a shadcn registry, so it lands in your project as source and belongs to you.

Who it's for

Designers who ship code

People building side projects, prototypes and odd little interfaces who want parts they can bend.

The promise

Copy it in. It's yours.

No package to upgrade and no theme to fight. One command, then the file is yours to change.

What's different

Two shelves

A plain base set, and a shelf of special components built for one moment: a chapter scrubber, reactions, a pixel snail loader.

One line for bios: An achromatic design system for experimental projects. shadcn registry, Base UI, Storybook.

01

Tensions

fibo sits between opposites and never settles on one side. Six pairs set the character of everything it makes. Each image holds both sides of a pair, split on the golden section and joined through a ragged band of pixel cells, so the two trade places cell by cell instead of meeting at a line.

hard ↔ soft Crisp pixel edges against soft focus. The rabbit is drawn to the pixel; the photos are grainy, pale and blurred.
mathematical ↔ organic Every layout is cut on 0.618, sizes follow the sequence, and the subjects are grown things. Here, 610 seeds placed by the golden angle, laid in white over the real seed head they describe.
analog ↔ digital Film grain and 1-bit dither in the same picture. Photos keep their grain; the texture is pure on or off.
ancient ↔ futuristic A puzzle from 1202 and a design system installed from a registry. The rabbit is from Liber Abaci; the parts install with one command.
precise ↔ whimsical The ratio to ten places, and a rabbit mid-hop. The brand speaks exactly; the rabbit gets the jokes.
black/white ↔ luminous color The system is ink and paper, with no brand hue. Color arrives only as light in a photograph, and when it does it glows.

Sections here are numbered along the sequence, which starts 1, 1: that is why there are two 01s.

02

How fibo talks

There are two voices. The brand speaks plainly in sentence case: short sentences, concrete nouns, no hype. fibo the rabbit speaks in lowercase deadpan one-liners, usually about being poked or about multiplying. Keep them apart: the brand explains, the rabbit heckles.

Rules

Sentence case everywhere. No emojis, in posts or replies. One hashtag at most, and only where the platform needs it. Link to the docs, not a landing page.

Numbering

Posts are numbered along the sequence: No. 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 and on. It's the same trick the principles on the welcome page use, and the numbers get big fast, which is part of the joke. This page's sections are numbered the same way.

The construction

The golden rectangle as hairlines: the cuts, the squares and the dashed diagonals, with no curve drawn through them. Always behind the copy, never over the headline at full strength.

The dotted grid

Every card sits on the same dot grid as the docs. It makes a post read as fibo before anyone reads a word.

The rabbit

20 × 20 pixels, one color, a one-pixel line with a white fill. On dark grounds he turns solid light with his lines cut in. Scale by whole multiples. Never smooth him, thicken his lines or give him a color.

05

The golden system

The name comes from Fibonacci, so the content leans on the math. Four devices, each used on purpose rather than as decoration.

Sizes on the sequence

Every size is a Fibonacci number

1321345589

Type on cards runs 21, 34, 55, 89, 144, 233. Gaps run 8 to 89. Pixel characters scale by 3, 5, 8, 13 or 21. Each step is about 1.618 times the last, so it's a golden-ratio scale in whole pixels.

The tiling

Squares of 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8

A layout grid for cards with more than one thing in them. The biggest idea gets the 8, the next the 5, and so on down.

The golden angle

137.5°, as a texture

Square pixel seeds, each turned 137.5° from the last. It's the alternative to the dot grid: use it behind one card in a set, never behind text.

The golden split

1 to 0.618

10.618

When a card has copy on one side and a picture on the other, split it 1 to 0.618 like the hero on the docs, with a dimension line drawn in the margin to show it.

08

Logo and mascot

The wordmark is Geist Medium at its own spacing. Its ascender, the top of the f and b, is the rabbit's height divided by 1.618, so the name holds its own beside the rabbit's dense pixels. The rabbit stands on the wordmark's baseline.

fibo is a rabbit: the name comes from Fibonacci, and the sequence came from rabbits. One color, crisp pixels, whole-number scaling. The brand stays plain; the rabbit gets the jokes.

fibo

Rabbit · 20 × 20

fibo is a rabbit, from the pair in Fibonacci's 1202 puzzle that started the sequence. Tall ears with inner lines, a round face, a haunch and a tail loop, in a one-pixel line.

there were two of us in 1202. it got out of hand.

Nature that follows the golden ratio, like spirals in stone, shells and seed heads, appears in photographs, not as characters. The pixel snail stays in the design system as a loading animation, but the brand is the rabbit’s.

13

Image treatment

Pixel texture and dither laid over dreamy photographs of nature, cut along the golden ratio. The pictures are soft and pale, like the painted rabbit. The texture is sampled from each picture's own light, kept light over the image, and thins out until it becomes the paper of the frame, so there is no edge between picture and page.

pixel texture: glyph cells, 13pxdither: 1-bit dots, 4px

Color comes from the photo

There is still no brand hue. Texture is ink on paper; the only color is the picture, graded soft and pale.

Cut on the ratio

Where the texture starts and stops is always a golden cut: a 0.618 line, a golden rectangle, the Fibonacci squares, or bands in proportion.

The signature

Every poster carries fibo and the address in the foot. Some add one character, small, in the middle of the foot.
